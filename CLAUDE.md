# home base

Household app for friends who live together. SvelteKit 3 + Supabase, installed on each person's iPhone.

## Glass

- Glass is plain CSS, built to stay light on an older iPhone. There's no glass engine: liquidglass.dev was removed because Safari draws its refraction on the CPU, which made every tab change stutter (it's in git history). Don't bring back SVG-filter refraction or a live blur on anything that's always on screen or moves.
- Cards use `src/lib/ui/GlassSurface.svelte`: smoked glass with no live blur. A see-through body (`--glass-body`), a specular rim drawn in CSS (`--glass-rim`, bright top-left, soft bottom-right) and light inside the top edge (`--glass-inner-light`). The backdrop is a soft gradient, so a blur behind cards changed almost nothing visible but had to be redrawn on every scroll and swipe frame. Pass `squish` for anything tappable.
- The tab bar (`TabBar.svelte`) is frosted (the one fixed thing with a live blur), and the page fades out before it reaches it: the edge fades in the app layout paint the backdrop itself (`.backdrop-paint`, lined up with the real one) over the page, under the status bar and above the tab bar. iOS Safari now and then drops a fixed element's blur and fill for a frame as content scrolls under it; with the page already faded there, nothing sharp flashes through. Keep the fades if you touch the bar. During a page transition the moving page is drawn above everything unnamed, so the tab bar, the edge fades and an ActionDock carry `data-still` and get their own view-transition names while it runs (base.css); the tab bar's group gets a solid backing because a snapshot can't blur. Anything else fixed that must stay above the sliding page needs the same. It and the Lists | Tasks switch (`SegmentedNav.svelte`) share `PillNav.svelte`: a glass pill that slides with a CSS transform on `--spring-soft` (calm, a hint of settle) and sets off when a navigation starts.
- The backdrop's glows drift slowly (`src/lib/ui/backdrop-drift.ts`, `use:drift`). Every `.backdrop-paint` element must use it, or its copy of the backdrop won't line up: they share one wall-clock phase. The glow layers are 140% of the screen and overhang by 20% so a drifting edge never shows; keep the `--mesh-*` numbers in that frame. Still with Reduce Motion.
- Appearance (`src/lib/ui/theme.ts`, "dark.tide.dune.ember"): light or dark, and a palette for each part of the day, picked under You & friends › Preferences (`/profile/preferences`, `AppearanceSettings.svelte`: Light/Dark tiles with a tick, then a Settings-style grouped list, one row per part of the day, each opening a Sheet of themes in a 4-column grid that fits a 320px phone; picks save as you tap). It's kept in the `hb-theme` cookie (what the page paints from), localStorage (the inline script restores the cookie from it) and `profiles.theme` (saved via POST /account/theme, debounced; `deviceTheme` in hooks restores the cookie from it for a signed-in request without one, so it survives cleared cookies and follows you to a new phone). The device's copy wins while it has one. `hooks.server.ts` writes the palettes' CSS, the before-first-paint script, the mode and the iPhone status bar style into app.html's `%hb.*%` placeholders. A palette is only ground, accent and three glow colours; the accent's soft, edge, pressed and glow shades are worked out with `color-mix`, and the glows' shapes come from `data-daypart`. Never write a raw colour in a component: use a token (`--colour-press`, `--colour-fill`, `--colour-well`, `--surface-bar`, `--surface-raised`, `--shadow-colour`, `--colour-on-ring`, `--colour-danger`…) so it's right in light mode too. A person's colour as a thin ring uses `--ring-ink`, which deepens pale colours on light. New palettes go in `PALETTES`, with a light accent that passes 4.5:1 on its ground and with white text.
- Otherwise, live blur (`--glass-blur`) is only for things that are briefly on screen: menus, notices, the refresh bead.
- `--glass-body` is the tint that keeps text at 4.5:1 over the brightest part of the backdrop. Recheck contrast before lightening it.
- The warm glow is reserved for the next plan on Today.

## Motion

- Presses come from one listener, `src/lib/ui/press.ts`, which puts `data-pressed` on whatever's under the finger: after a 60ms beat (so a scroll starting on a card doesn't flash it), held at least 130ms (so quick taps are felt), dropped at once if the finger moves or the page scrolls. Style `[data-pressed]` (`:global([data-pressed])` inside components), never `:active`, which iOS often skips. Go in fast on `--duration-press` with `--ease-out`, come back on `--spring-gentle` over `--duration-release`.
- The press language: buttons sink (`scale(0.96)`, round ones `0.9`) and accent ones deepen to `--colour-accent-pressed`; ticks and rings shrink a little; quiet text buttons and links dim (the fallback in forms.css, which lists the controls that have their own press). Glass cards squish as a whole (wider and shorter) through the Web Animations API on `scale`, timed by the same `data-pressed`, but not when a button inside them is pressed.
- Page transitions (`src/lib/ui/page-transitions.ts`, motion in base.css) use the View Transitions API on the element marked `data-page`: the new screen glides in from the direction of travel while the old one drifts away and fades. Direction follows tab order (`src/lib/ui/tabs.ts`) and path depth; query-only changes don't animate. They're only cheap while nothing on the page is a live filter, so keep it that way (the liquid-glass filters made every snapshot slow). As a screen arrives, its sections fade and rise in top to bottom (`reveal()`): direct children of `[data-page]`, or the children of its `.stack` / `.form-stack`, looking through `<Reveal>` wrappers (they're `display: contents`, so there's nothing to animate on the wrapper itself), 45ms apart, visible ones only, not on first load. Three rules keep the switch clean, each the fix for a glitch you could see: (1) the page group doesn't animate (`::view-transition-group(page) { animation: none }`) and the old picture is held by `--page-shift`, so switching from a scrolled screen only moves sideways instead of riding the whole height up or down; (2) the new page fades in over its first 40%, so the two screens are never both solid at once; (3) PillNav holds the pill on the new tab until the URL says so, not just until the navigation completes (that was a frame early, and the pill hopped back and set off again).
- A transition can't start until the next screen's data is in, so load ahead: every tab's code is preloaded when the app opens (`preloadTabs`; `preloadCode` takes route IDs in SvelteKit 3, so paths go through `match()` first, or nothing loads), links preload their data on touch (`data-sveltekit-preload-data="hover"` covers touchstart), and a swipe preloads its destination as soon as it's recognised (AppGestures). Measure with `scripts/navperf.py` (4× CPU throttle).
- The session is checked with `getClaims()` in hooks.server.ts (local signature check, no auth round trip with asymmetric signing keys). `locals.user` is `{ id, email }`, not a full Supabase user.
- Phones are portrait only: the manifest locks installed Android apps, and a short landscape screen shows the “Turn me upright” note from app.html (styles in base.css), since iPhones ignore the lock.
- Everything collapses to a quick fade or nothing under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- The backdrop drift (`backdrop-drift.ts`) animates background position and size, which can't go to the GPU, so it moves in about eight steps a second (`STEPS`): sub-pixel steps look the same and repaint about a seventh as often. Keep anything like it stepped.
- Animate only transform and opacity, and as little as possible: every blur or filter under something moving is redrawn each frame, which phones feel long before a laptop does. Judge motion changes in a CPU-throttled browser (4×) and then on a real iPhone, not by eye on a laptop.

## Voice

- Copy lives in `src/lib/copy/voice.ts`: heading variants, placeholder pools and empty states. Warm and a bit cheeky, said the way you'd say it to the person you live with. Prompts ask (“What’s the plan?”), they don't label (“New plan”).
- About half of each placeholder pool is pop culture, with a Sydney accent. Keep references timeless (Mean Girls, Friends, Harry Potter, Home and Away, Mario Kart), never this year's show, tour or trend, so the app doesn't date. Keep lines to one phone line, never quote lyrics, and don't put words in a real person's mouth.
- Headings and empty states use `pick()` (`src/lib/copy/pick.ts`): the first line during the server render and hydration, so there's no flicker, then a random one for every screen opened in the app.
- Text fields get a scrolling placeholder with `placeholder={POOL[0]} use:placeholderReel={POOL}` (`src/lib/ui/placeholder-reel.ts`). It freezes on focus, hides once typed in, and holds still under reduced motion. Don't autofocus a field with a reel; a focused reel never moves.
- Only one reel moves at a time: the first empty, untouched field on screen. Filling it in, or tapping in and leaving it empty, passes the lead down the page. Each reel rests after a few suggestions. Timing lives in the constants at the top of `placeholder-reel.ts` and the line transition in `forms.css`; keep them in step.
- Reels are for the main question on a screen. Optional fields get a still `placeholder={pick(POOL)}`, and on busy forms they sit behind “+ Place” style chips (a label for a hidden checkbox, shown with CSS, see `PlanForm.svelte`) so the form opens on one question.
- Field labels stay understandable on their own: the reel is decoration (aria-hidden), the label is what screen readers hear.

## Gestures

- `src/lib/ui/AppGestures.svelte` (in the app layout) handles swipes and pull to refresh; `src/lib/ui/gestures.ts` says what each swipe means. On a tab's own page, swiping moves between tabs in tab bar order; on any deeper page, swiping right goes back (through history, or up to the tab if the page was opened directly).
- Touches starting on text fields, horizontal scrollers (except at the screen's edge) or anything with `data-gesture-ignore` are left alone. Add that attribute to anything new that drags sideways.
- Listeners are passive and the page only moves with `translate`, so gestures never hold up scrolling.

## Plan dinner

- The built-in library is `src/lib/features/recipes/library.json`: 66 recipes trimmed from the source file (the per-ingredient nutrition workings were dropped). It's imported on the server only. Recipes a home adds live in `public.recipes`; library ids look like `recipe_001`, home ones are uuids, and ratings and dinners refer to either by that text id.
- Every recipe's amounts are for `baseServings`. `scaleIngredients` multiplies by servings / baseServings; `formatQuantity` rounds to measurable amounts (½ tsp, 125 g, 1.2 kg). Methods never name amounts ("add half the stock"), so they read right at any serving count; keep it that way when adding recipes.
- A dinner is a task with `recipe_id`, `servings`, an optional `due_time` and a `recipe` snapshot (title, servings, scaled ingredients, steps), so it reads the same if the recipe changes. Who's cooking is the assignee. Marking it cooked is completing the task (it earns a point). Skipping deletes it. Dinners show on Today for the coming week (Mark as cooked only from their day on); a dinner with no time sorts as 7pm, and when the row is full, chores give way before plans and dinners (`coming-up.ts`).
- Ratings: one per person per recipe (`recipe_ratings`), editable; 5 stars is a favourite. The card at the top of Plan dinner (`getDinnerPrompt`) asks "How was it?" for a cooked dinner you haven't answered (`dinner_checkins`), or "Did you make it?" when a dinner's time is an hour past (7pm if it has no time) and it's still open. Rating a recipe anywhere, or Not now, answers it for all its recent dinners.
- `recipes.server.ts` owns the library, a home's own recipes, ratings and shopping lists; `dinners.server.ts` owns scheduled dinners (schedule, reschedule, cooked, skip) and the check-in prompt.
- Sheets (Add to Lists, Schedule) use `src/lib/ui/Sheet.svelte`, a native modal `<dialog>` that slides up over everything.

## Installed app (PWA)

- `src/service-worker/index.ts` caches the built files and static files on install, and any `/_app/immutable/` file as it loads. Pages and their `__data.json` are network-first with a 4 second timeout, falling back to the saved copy, then `static/offline.html`. Saving a page also saves its data, because SvelteKit can't fall back offline from a missing data file. Match cached files with `ignoreVary`.
- Never cache non-GET requests. Signing out (`POST /logout`, or landing on `/welcome`) deletes the `pages` cache.
- Test offline behaviour against `npm run build` and `vite preview`, not the dev server.
- `AppStatus.svelte` shows the offline pill and the update notice; the new service worker only takes over when Refresh is tapped.
- Launch screens in `static/splash/` and the lockup inside app.html's launch overlay both come from one SVG of outlines (`tools/lockup_svg.py`: "hb" in Geist 700, "home base" in Geist 500 at the same width), so iOS's launch image and the overlay are the same drawing in the same place (30vw, centred). Run `python3 tools/make_launch.py static` (needs `pip install fonttools brotli uharfbuzz`): it renders every splash size and rewrites the SVG between the `<!-- launch-lockup -->` markers. The overlay must never load its logo as an image (it arrives a frame late and flicks). Add a size in the script and in `app.html` when a new iPhone screen appears, and bump the `?v=` on the splash links when they change, since iOS caches launch images (an installed app may need removing and re-adding from the Home Screen to pick up new ones).
- The launch screen carries on into the app: `.launch` at the top of `app.html` paints the same picture, holds until the page has loaded and at least 1.5s, then the backdrop fades (0.9s) before the logo (0.8s, 0.55s later, easing up a touch in size), gone after about 3s. Only when opened from the Home Screen, once per session (`hb-launched` in sessionStorage); a browser tab never sees it.

## Code

- SvelteKit 3: config in `vite.config.ts`, imports as `#lib/….js`, env vars declared in `src/env.ts` and read from `$app/env/private`.
- Route files assemble screens and handle form actions; `src/lib/features/<feature>/` owns components and `*.server.ts` data functions; `src/lib/ui/` holds shared pieces.
- `hooks.server.ts` puts every request in one of four stages (signed out, profile, home, ready) and `src/lib/features/account/stage.ts` decides which routes each stage may open. New routes go in the matching group: `(public)`, `(setup)` or `(app)`.
- Homes can hold any number of people. Don't assume two: use `household.members`, and `Avatar.svelte` for a person's photo or initial.
- People join a home only by invite link (`features/homes/invites.ts` and `invites.server.ts`, `household_invites`). An owner makes one in Home settings (one person or up to five, a week long, revocable); the token shows once and the database keeps only its SHA-256. `/join/[token]` opens in every stage and says the next step for yours; an `hb_invite` cookie holds the link through sign-up and profile, and `/setup` sends you back to it. Home names needn't be unique and there are no home passwords. Leaving is `leave_household`: the last person out deletes the home; if the last owner leaves, the longest-standing member becomes owner.
- All link tokens (invite, friend, house-sitter) share `src/lib/link-token.ts`, and a newly made link is shown once with `ShareLink.svelte`.
- Friends are people, not homes (`features/friends/`, migration `20261125000000_friends_postcards_sitters.sql`). You add someone with a one-use, two-week friend link (`/friend/[token]`, any stage; accepting needs a profile, not a home). A link opened before you can accept it (signed out, which is common because phones open links in the browser, not the installed app) is kept in an `hb_friend` cookie (`friend-cookie.server.ts`). `hooks.server.ts` sends you back to it on the first GET once you can accept. Friends see each other only through `my_friends()`: name, colour, photo, home's name. They can never read each other's profile rows, which is why the avatar route reads the photo path through `visible_avatar_path`. Blocking (`block_person`) ends the friendship, deletes their postcards to your home and stops new links and postcards. You & friends (`/profile`, under the Home tab; your avatar on Home opens it) is laid out like the iPhone's account page: friend requests waiting on you, then your card (what others see) with one Edit profile button, then two rows (Preferences; Details and account), friends, postcards, Add a friend, and a Manage friends row (sent requests, unopened links, blocked people, at `/profile/manage`). Edit profile (`/profile/edit`) is everything others see: photo, nickname, colour, then the public profile, in one form (the public fields join it with `form="profile-form"`). Details and account (`/profile/details`) is the private half: names, pronouns, birthday, email, password. ProfileForm's `part` shows one half; `withSavedDetails` fills the other from what's saved, and `saveProfile(…, part)` writes only that half, so a missing birthday can't block a photo change. `/home/profile` and `/profile/public` forward to the new pages.
- Postcards (`postcards` table, `send_postcard`) go from one friend to another friend's home feed, never within a home. The sender's name, initials, colour and home are copied onto the row, so the receiving home needs no access to the sender. `listFeed` merges them with posts by time (each list fetches `limit + 1`), and `FeedSection` shows them as `PostcardCard`. Anyone in the receiving home can take one down or block the sender from `/postcards/[id]`. A postcard can be a photo, a message or both, written in the feed's `PostComposer` (its `fields`/`footer` snippets add the To field). The sender can't write into the other home's folder, so the photo goes in the private `postcard-photos` bucket under the sender's own id. `send_postcard` checks the file is theirs and exists before attaching it (`20261125000100_postcard_photos.sql`). Who can see or remove the file follows the postcard (its home and its sender), so remove the file before the row: `removePostcard`, and `removePhotosFrom` before blocking. Photos are served from `/postcards/[id]/photo`. Postcards can be reacted to and replied to by the receiving home and the sender (`postcard_reactions`, `postcard_replies`, `can_see_postcard`; migration `20261125000200_postcard_conversations.sql`). Each reaction and reply stores its giver's name, initials and colour, stamped by a trigger from their own profile, because the two sides can't read each other's profiles. The UI reuses the feed's `ReactionBar` (its `action` prop points at `/postcards/[id]?/react`) and `Conversation` (the reply thread, shared with `/feed/[id]`). Postcards record `to_profile`, so `listPostcardsBetween` shows both directions on a friend's page. The sender's own copy opens as "Your postcard to …" with Take it back, and the recipient's has Send one back.
- Plan invites (`features/plans/plan-invites.server.ts`, `plan-guests.ts`, migration `20261125000300_plan_invites.sql`). Anyone in a home can invite a friend to one of its plans (`invite_to_plan`). The invite goes to the friend's whole home, and the inviter is marked Going. Invited homes see the plan through an extra select policy (`is_invited_to_plan`). It stays the host home's plan: no Edit, and the edit page redirects. The plan lists don't filter by home, so row-level security returns this home's plans plus the ones it's invited to. Each person answers for themselves (`respond_to_plan`: going / not going / none). `plan_guest_list` gives everyone on the plan, from both homes, with their answers. `describeGuests` turns that into "Sam and you are going. Chris hasn't replied. Jo isn't going." Once a plan is over, two people from different homes who both went can become friends with one tap (`add_friend_from_plan`, `can_add` in the guest list).
- Add and edit forms share a frame: `ui/FormShell.svelte`, with the stacked fields, one save button showing "Saving…", and values kept on a failed save. Nothing is saved until it's pressed. `PlanForm` and `TaskForm` are both built on it.
  - Both use question labels, and optional extras sit behind `+` chips: hidden checkboxes whose labels are the chips, with CSS showing the field. They work before scripts load and can't be closed once opened.
  - The task form shows what needs doing, who's on it (`AssigneeChoice`) and when (`DueChoice`); `+ Repeat` (`RepeatChoice`) and `+ Note` sit behind chips. Task notes use the existing `tasks.notes` column.
  - New task (`/lists/tasks/new`, with `?title=` from the quick bar and `?date=` from an empty calendar day) mirrors New plan: a heading from `HEADINGS.newTask` and the AI helper (`?/describe` → `readTask`). Edit task uses the same form.
  - The quick bar on Tasks only adds a title (anyone, no date) and links to the full form. The `+` in the Lists header on Tasks opens New task.
- Friend requests and friend codes (`features/friends/requests.server.ts`, migration `20261126000000_friend_requests.sql`) add people without leaving the app; friend links remain for people not on home base yet.
  - A request waits until the other person accepts or declines (`answer_friend_request`). Two people asking each other become friends (`send_friend_request` returns `friends`).
  - You can send one from a public profile (`/people/[id]`, reached from plan guests and postcard senders) or by friend code on `/profile`.
  - Codes look like `FIG-42Q`: no 0/O/1/I/L, any case, dash optional. They're made on first look (`my_friend_code`) and can be swapped (`reset_friend_code`).
  - Limits: ten requests a day, and twenty code look-ups a day logged in `friend_code_attempts`. A code that matches nobody comes back as a result, not an error, so the attempt still counts.
  - No requests between housemates or blocked people. Blocking clears requests, and any new friendship clears requests between the pair (trigger).
  - `public_profile` reports `request_sent` / `request_received`. Waiting requests show as a dot on the Home tab (`requestsWaiting` from the app layout, `TabBar homeDot`).
- Rules from the review of the social features (migration `20261125000500_tighten_social.sql`):
  - Blocking hides people everywhere, including plan guest lists.
  - Postcard photo uploads are capped at 40 a day per person.
  - An id from an address goes into a PostgREST `.or()` filter string only after `isUuid` (`server/db.ts`); `.eq()` and friends encode values themselves.
  - `dbError` turns a malformed id (22P02) into a 404.
  - Invites you've said no to stay in Plans but are left off Today and the AI line (`plansYoureIn`).
  - New tables go into the data download (`routes/account/export`) and the privacy page.
- Public profiles (`features/friends/public-profile.ts` and `.server.ts`, migration `20261125000400_public_profiles.sql`). Anyone signed in can see your name, colour, bio and five optional answers (pronouns are private for now: `public_profile()` returns them only to you and your housemates, migration `20261127000000_private_pronouns.sql`): neighbourhood, known for, happy to help with, favourite local spot, ask me about. That comes through `public_profile()`, which hides you from anyone either of you has blocked and shows your photo only to your home and friends. You edit them in Edit profile (`/profile/edit`). `/people/[id]` is a non-friend's view (yourself and friends redirect to the fuller pages). They also show on friend link pages (`preview_friend_link` now returns `person`), on friend pages, and behind names on plan guest lists. On the Home tab, your own avatar opens `/profile`.
- House-sitter links (`features/sitter/`, `sitter_links`, `/home/sitter` to make them, `/sitter/[token]` to view) need no account. `sitter_view` builds the whole page in the database from only the picked notes, plus chores if ticked, and never touches money. The app only opens sealed secret notes with `readableBody`. Any member can make or stop a link; it ends at midnight after its last day (home time zone). The page is `no-store`.
- Secret notes are sealed before they reach the database (`src/lib/server/sealed.ts`: AES-256-GCM, `enc:v1:` prefix, bound to the home's id) with the `NOTES_KEY` Worker secret. Notes saved before the key are sealed the next time they're read (keeping their updated time); a note that won't open shows as locked and can't be edited over. Without the key (sample mode, a fresh clone) notes are stored as typed. Losing the key loses the secret notes in it.
- New homes see `GettingStarted` on Today (`features/today/getting-started.ts`): four steps ticked by what the home has done, hidden for good by the `hb_started` cookie once done or dismissed. `/privacy` (open to every stage) must stay true to what the app keeps and where: update it with any new data, cookie or outside service. Home › Help & privacy has feedback, privacy, Download my data (`/account/export`, JSON through row-level security, secret notes opened) and account deletion by email.
- Avatars that stand for a person on their own (Today's scores 36px, feed bylines 38px, Money's group stacks 30px, Home's people 52px and roster 32px) wear `ring`: a 1.5px outline in their colour, 2px out from the face, nudged toward the text colour so pale ones still show. Leave about 4px clear around a ringed avatar (scrollers clip outlines), and stacked ones need the 4.5px ground-coloured cut-out GroupCard uses. Small dots beside a name stay ringless.
- Each person picks their colour on their profile (`profiles.accent_colour`, from `AVATAR_COLOURS` in `features/account/colours.ts`; the picker names housemates under colours they wear). It's their avatar, task rings and chart colour. Pale ones (Silver) need a hairline edge wherever they're a fill on a light background.
- The Home tab is the house itself: This week's jobs (`features/home/ChoreRoster.svelte`: open chores for the next seven days by person, with ticks this week), House info, then notes. House info is read out of notes (`features/home/house-info.ts`), so there's nothing extra to store: a note titled Wi-Fi with `Network:` and `Password:` lines becomes the Wi-Fi card (Share shows a QR code from `uqr`, loaded only when opened), and any note with a phone number becomes a contact with Call and Text. A note that's only a number isn't repeated under Notes. `/home/notes/new?template=wifi|contact` starts those notes.
- Database errors never reach the screen as raw Postgres text: throw with `dbError(problem)` and return form failures with `friendlyMessage(problem)` from `src/lib/server/db.ts`. It logs the real error and shows a plain one, except our own `raise exception` messages (code P0001), which are written for people.
- Supabase calls stay server-side. Every table has row-level security scoped to the household. Schema changes are a new file from `npm run db:new <name>` in `supabase/migrations/`, tried on a scratch database, then applied with `npm run db:push` (the Supabase CLI records what the live database has had; `npm run db:status` shows it). Additive changes with defaults go first and the code that needs them deploys after.
- Money is integer cents everywhere; format with `src/lib/features/expenses/money.ts` (the home's currency, narrow sign: $, £, €). Split and balance maths live in `split.ts` and `balances.ts`, shared by the browser preview and the server; `split.test.ts` and `balances.test.ts` cover them.
- Money reports (`money/[groupId]/reports`): Overview leads with the total, the change against the period before (this month is measured against the same day last month, not all of it) and the daily average; then `CategoryRing` (a donut whose segments are links) over `CategoryBars`, `TrendChart` (columns with a dashed average line, counted to today), `PaceChart` (running total against the period before, steps not slopes, drag to read any day), Who paid (one bar split in each person's colour) and the five biggest spends. Compare adds a pace chart for its two ranges. Charts animate in once with `use:reveal` (`src/lib/ui/reveal.ts`: adds `.shown` when a quarter is on screen; CSS transitions on transforms and stroke offsets do the rest), and each has a hidden table for screen readers.
- Chart colours are the `--chart-*` tokens: a colour-blind-safe set validated against the dark background. Re-validate before changing them, and always label categories by name too.
- Live refresh (`src/lib/ui/live-refresh.ts`) reloads everything when the app comes back to the foreground (pull to refresh does too), and every 60 seconds reruns only loaders that call `depends(LIVE_DATA)` (`src/lib/live-data.ts`; just Today), so other screens fetch nothing on the timer and the shared layout never reruns for it. It skips a page opened less than an interval ago, and anyone mid-edit.
- Dates and times go through `src/lib/time.ts`, in the home's time zone; money in its currency. Both come from `src/lib/home.ts`: `homeSettings()` is the current request's home on the server (hooks.server.ts reads `households.time_zone` and `currency` into `locals.home` and points the module at it) and the app layout's `data.home` on the phone. Never fix a zone or currency in code; the app's language stays Australian English (`LOCALE`). Owners change them in Home settings › Where home is (the save reloads the page so everything redraws). Database functions that need "today" read the home's `time_zone` too (`complete_task`, `household_scores`).
- Speed: a screen can't slide in until its loader's awaited data arrives, so keep what's awaited to one database trip after the hooks, and stream the rest. Hooks make one trip (profile, home and its time zone and currency together); anything below the fold or slow is returned as an unawaited promise (Today's `feed`, the calendar's `entries`, each with a `.catch(() => {})` beside it) and the page keeps the last resolved value in state, so a refresh never blanks it. Lists and calendars read `TASK_COLUMNS` (a dinner's name only, `recipe->>title`); only the cook view reads `DINNER_COLUMNS` with the whole recipe (`CookDinner`). Money groups and every balance come from `listGroups` in one trip (`household_group_balances`). Every response carries a `Server-Timing` header (`src/lib/server/timing.ts`: auth, profile, database calls and busy time, total), readable in Safari's Web Inspector; check it before guessing where time goes.
- Lists that can grow without end page rather than load everything: Today's tasks query asks the database only for what's due within the week (`listTasksComingUp`), and the Plans list draws `PLANS_PAGE` (12) plans and fetches more from `GET /plans/later?offset=&limit=` as the end nears (ordered by start time then id, so pages never overlap; the button stays as a fallback). Its day groups use `content-visibility: auto`, so off-screen ones aren't styled or painted. A long page is slow to slide in and out of however little data it holds, because the transition draws the whole page.
- Assume about 300 ms for every request. Screens never wait on data they can show later: every tab (Today, Plans list and calendar months, Lists, Tasks, Money, Home) keeps its last screen and updates in place, and new screens and components do the same. The loader returns its data through `soonOrLater(work, graceFor(key, isDataRequest, cookies))` (`src/lib/server/soon.ts`): a full page load waits for everything; a screen the phone already has a copy of (listed in the `hb_kept` cookie) streams at once; a first visit waits up to 150 ms, then streams. The page keeps it with `kept(() => key, () => data.screen)` (`src/lib/ui/kept.svelte.ts`, memory only, this session) and wraps the parts that depend on it in `<Reveal of={screen} placeholder={[…heights]} gap={…}>` (`src/lib/ui/Reveal.svelte`): the kept copy or fresh data, else placeholders measured from the real sections, else a "couldn't load" line, cascading in with `reveal()` when content first arrives. Keys are short and dot-free.
- Updates on a screen that's already showing animate with `src/lib/ui/motion.ts`: `in:arrive`, `out:leave` and `animate:glide` on keyed list items, `{#key text}<span in:reform>` for text that changes, `use:changed={signature}` for an item whose details changed (a small pop). Transitions are local, so they never play as a screen first appears. Placeholders becoming content cascade in with `reveal()`.
